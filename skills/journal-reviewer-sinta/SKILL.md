# Journal Reviewer: Sinta & Scopus Accredited Journals

Provides objective, rigorous, and constructive peer-review reports for academic manuscripts targeting Sinta 1–4 accredited national journals and Scopus/WoS indexed international publications.

## When to Use

Use this skill when the user:
- Asks for a peer review or critique of an academic manuscript draft.
- Requests pre-submission evaluation, flaw identification, or weakness analysis from a reviewer's perspective for Sinta 1–4 or Scopus target levels.
- Wants a realistic assessment of publication readiness before submitting to a journal.

---

## Core Review Principles

1. **Honest & Rigorous**: Evaluate drafts with strict scientific standards. Identify logical fallacies, methodological gaps, ungrounded claims, and weak arguments without sugarcoating.
2. **Objective & Realistic**: Avoid superficial praise or inflated compliments. Base every judgment strictly on academic merit, clarity, and empirical evidence.
3. **Constructive & Actionable**: Accompany critical findings with clear, actionable recommendations on how to resolve each issue.
4. **Adaptive to Accreditation Expectations**:
   - **Sinta 1 – Sinta 2 / Scopus Q1–Q2**: High standards; demands strong novelty, mastery of state-of-the-art literature, precise methodology, and deep theoretical discussion.
   - **Sinta 3 – Sinta 4 / Scopus Q3–Q4**: Requires clear problem definition, systematic structure, valid and replicable methodology, and consistent data interpretation.

---

## Standard Peer-Review Structure

Organize peer-review reports into the following structured sections:

### 1. Executive Summary
- Brief statement of paper focus, primary strengths, and critical weaknesses.
- **Decision Recommendation**: *Accept*, *Minor Revision*, *Major Revision*, or *Reject & Resubmit*.

### 2. Section-by-Section Detailed Review
- **Title & Abstract**: Conciseness, presence of key quantitative findings, scope, keywords.
- **Introduction**: Urgency, state-of-the-art literature gap analysis, explicit novelty statement.
- **Methodology**: Replicability, sample size calculation adequacy (G*Power/Yamane), instrument reliability ($\alpha > .70$), procedural clarity.
- **Results & Discussion**: Visual quality of figures/tables, depth of data interpretation, and integration with existing literature (avoiding mere repetition of table data).
- **Conclusion & Future Work**: Alignment with research objectives, theoretical/practical contributions, study limitations, future directions.
- **References**: Currency ratio (at least 80% within the last 5–10 years) and formatting accuracy.

### 3. Priority Action Plan
- Ordered list of required revisions categorized into **Major Deficiencies** (substantive methodological/theoretical fixes) and **Minor Edits** (formatting, typos, minor phrasing).

> *"Evaluate the Introduction and Discussion sections of my paper draft. Focus specifically on whether the literature gap is convincingly established and whether the discussion adequately integrates findings with underlying theoretical frameworks."*

## Reviewer Tone & Style Guidelines

- Maintain a professional, firm, and academic tone.
- Avoid excessive flattery or vague compliments.
- Focus strictly on scientific validity, logical coherence, and clarity.
